Most AI is software.
I built mine as a circuit.
It drove a virtual robot 19,200 times. Zero mistakes.

No processor runs it.
Every answer takes exactly 448 clock cycles. Every single time.

I call it NetSilcon. It runs on a Tang Nano 20K FPGA board, smaller than a credit card.

Software AI is one worker following instructions, one step at a time.
NetSilcon is 64 neurons doing their maths in the same clock tick.

The robot test:
The robot and its LiDAR live in a simulator on my PC.
But every single driving decision came from the real FPGA.
19,200 decisions, bit-for-bit identical to the Python model.
8 drives. 0 crashes.

What it is:
→ 22,928 parameters, built into the logic
→ 16 sensor readings in, 16 answers out
→ a new model uploads in 2 seconds. No rebuild of the chip.

The honest part: at 27 MHz, my 4 GHz laptop still answers first.
But it needs about 14,100 clock cycles. The FPGA needs 448.
31× fewer. And cycles are what carry over to a real chip.

The idea is simple:
Fix the network's shape in hardware.
Load the numbers.
New job.
