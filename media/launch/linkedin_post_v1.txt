My FPGA needs 31× fewer clock cycles than my laptop CPU.

NetSilcon started as one tiny neural network on a small FPGA board: 144 weights, an answer in 15 clock cycles.

The question I kept getting: does the idea scale?

Today I can answer it. Meet NetSilcon-64x6:

→ 22,528 int8 weights on the same Tang Nano 20K board
→ 448 clock cycles per decision. Every single time.
→ 3,200 test vectors, bit-for-bit identical to the Python model
→ Its first trained model drives a robot: 19,200 decisions with the real FPGA in the loop. 0 mismatches. 0 crashes.

Now the honest part.
My laptop runs at 4 GHz. The FPGA runs at 27 MHz. So the laptop finishes first.
But it needs about 14,100 clock cycles. The FPGA needs 448.

Cycles are what carry over to a real chip. And a chip is where NetSilcon is going.

The idea hasn't changed:
Fix the network's shape in hardware.
Load the numbers.
New job.
