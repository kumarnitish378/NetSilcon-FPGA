My FPGA needs 31× fewer clock cycles than my laptop CPU to run the same neural network.

Same int8 maths. Bit-for-bit identical answers.

Here's what I built, and the part where the laptop still wins 🧵
---
Honest part first.

Laptop: 4.09 GHz. FPGA: 27 MHz.
So the laptop finishes first: 3.45 µs vs 16.6 µs.

But the laptop burns ~14,100 cycles per answer. The FPGA needs 448.

Cycles are what carry over to a real chip.
---
The engine: NetSilcon-64x6

16 → 64×6 → 16, 22,528 int8 weights
32 block RAMs, 64 MAC lanes
One input broadcast per clock, one shared requantizer

All on a small Tang Nano 20K. 35% of its logic.
---
The idea: fix the network's shape in hardware, keep the numbers loadable.

New 23 KB model over USB in 2 s.
CRC-32 checked, swapped in one clock.
Damaged upload? Refused. The old model keeps running.

Same board, new job.
---
Its first job: driving a robot.

360° LiDAR → 16 sectors → speed + steering.
Trained only on random rooms.

Then the real FPGA drove, in the loop:
19,200 decisions
0 mismatches with Python
0 crashes
